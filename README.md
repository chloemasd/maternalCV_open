# Impacts of ambient temperature on pregnant women's cardiovascular function and variations related to fetal sex.

This is the code supporting the analyses.

Author: Chloé Masdoumier
Date: 2025-10-06

## Abstract
**Background** Rising global temperatures affect several aspects of health, especially for vulnerable populations such as pregnant women and newborns. Both heat and cold exposures have been linked to pregnancy complications. However, the impacts of temperature on maternal cardiovascular physiology and the potential windows of vulnerability remain largely unexplored. **Methods** We investigated the effects of subacute (0-28 days) heat and cold exposures, estimated using a cutting-edge spatiotemporal model, on heart rate, hematocrit levels, and repeated systolic and diastolic blood pressure measurements (transformed into gestaitonal age-specific Z-scores, zSBP, zDBP) for 1,854 pregnant women enrolled in the EDEN French cohort. We used distributed lag non-linear models to model both cumulative effects and lag-response relationship for heat (95th percentile; 20°C compared to 11°C) and cold (5th percentile; 4°C compared to 11°C). Models were stratified by gestational age, fetal sex, and accounted for co-exposures to air pollution, vegetation and humidity. **Results** Heat was associated with a sharp decrease in zSBP for up to 10 days after exposure (-0.14 SD [-0.20; -0.08] 95%-CI). Cold was associated with higher zSBP and hematocrit levels (0.06 SD [0.02; 0.09] and 0.28% [0.04; 0.51], respectively) over 2-6 days after exposure, and zSBP associations were observed only with female fetuses. Subacute effects of heat were not modified by gestational age, nor by air pollution and vegetation exposure, whereas cold effects were stronger when adjusted for air pollution and modified by low particulate matter exposure. **Conclusion** This study highlights how short-term heat and cold exposures affect the cardiovascular function of pregnant women, according to the sex of the fetus. These results provide insgihts into the biological plausibility of temperature extremes effects on pregnancy complications.
